Experience

From engineering execution to company-level responsibility

My experience is best understood as a progression: deeper technical ownership, broader business context, and increasing responsibility for people, decisions, and outcomes.

01

Early engineering and cloud experience

Building technical foundations

I began with software delivery and progressively expanded into infrastructure, cloud platforms, delivery automation, observability, container orchestration, and security. That breadth taught me to connect application behavior with the systems that support it.

02

Production ownership and operational maturity

Operating production systems

My responsibility grew into the day-to-day operation of business-critical services: reliability, databases, backups, incident response, security, performance, monitoring, automation, and controlled production change. I learned to work calmly when documentation or ownership was incomplete.

03

Company-level responsibility

Moving into technical leadership

I moved beyond executing isolated tasks to coordinating priorities with leadership, explaining risk and cost, mentoring engineers, working across teams and vendors, and remaining accountable for the operational result. The public title I use is Technology & Platform Leader; it is positioning, not a contractual title.

04

Education, mentoring, and talent access

Teaching and ecosystem building

At Leaders University, I teach and mentor across Cloud, DevOps, Cybersecurity, Data Science, and Big Data. Applied labs, workshops, project guidance, and academic collaboration have expanded my contribution from operating systems to developing people and early-stage opportunities.

What I bring

Technical judgment connected to business reality.

  • Ownership of ambiguous, business-critical environments
  • Reliable operations, security awareness, and calm incident response
  • Clear communication with technical and non-technical stakeholders
  • Mentoring, teaching, and knowledge transfer
  • Practical support for founding teams and early-stage validation
