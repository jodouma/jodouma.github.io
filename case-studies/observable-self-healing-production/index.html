Production engineering case study

Building Observable, Self-Healing Production

I connected application, host, database, security, domain, crawler and recording signals to bounded recovery—and traced a recurring Passenger failure beyond its runtime symptom to certificate renewal.

Context

On a high-traffic Rails platform, “the site is slow” or “production is down” can begin at Cloudflare, Nginx, Passenger, Rails, PostgreSQL, Redis, the host, a certificate or an external domain. When signals live in separate tools, operators either restart too much or lose time proving which boundary failed.

Problem

I needed to turn monitoring into an operational system: detect important security and reliability events, distinguish failure modes, route the right severity and owner, recover bounded failures automatically, and use each incident to remove the next recurring cause.

Architecture

New Relic and NRQL exposed application behavior; Grafana and Prometheus showed hosts, processes and dependencies; Loki and OpenSearch connected logs and security events; external checks covered service health, domains, certificates and blacklist risk; custom scripts watched production-specific conditions; team-chat routes separated normal follow-up from critical response.

Investigation

Passenger provided the clearest example of why correlation mattered. Recurring gateway errors and restarts looked like an application-runtime problem. I compared timing, socket/process state, Nginx behavior and certificate operations, and traced the repeating trigger to Certbot renewal behavior. A restart could hide the symptom; it could not remove the cause.

Implementation

I corrected the certificate lifecycle using Cloudflare DNS-01 where appropriate, controlled reload behavior, stabilized Passenger's operating path and added certificate/domain monitoring. Renewal, expiry and HTTP/HTTPS health became observable states instead of assumptions, so a future lifecycle problem could be caught before it surfaced as application unavailability.

Technical approach

  • Correlate the customer symptom with edge, application, process, database, cache and host signals.
  • Attach severity and ownership to the event, not only a threshold.
  • Retry external or service probes before escalating known false-positive paths.
  • Automate only bounded recovery whose preconditions and dependent services can be checked.
  • Convert the investigation into a monitor, runbook or permanent correction after recovery.

Validation

Every recovery or lifecycle change was checked at the boundary it affected and through the end-user path: configuration validity, process state, dependent services, application responses, certificate trust/expiry and external domain reachability. Alert retries and incident feedback reduced noise without weakening escalation.

Outcome

Production moved from fragmented dashboards and recurring manual recovery toward connected diagnosis, actionable alerts and bounded self-healing. Most importantly, one persistent Passenger problem stopped being “the service restarted again” and became a corrected, monitored certificate-lifecycle design.
