Case study · PostgreSQL operations

Reclaiming 66 GiB from a Production PostgreSQL Database

I contributed to production PostgreSQL optimization that reduced storage from approximately 199 GiB to 133 GiB, reclaiming 66 GiB (about 33%).

66 GiB

reclaimed

199 GiB → 133 GiB · about 33%

Context

A long-running international online marketplace was accumulating database storage pressure. I contributed to the production optimization work within a wider employer-owned, collaborative platform.

Problem

Growth had left a material difference between allocated storage and the space the active workload required. The challenge was to identify safely reclaimable space, reduce the footprint and avoid turning a storage intervention into an availability incident.

Constraints

  • The work affected a live relational database supporting an established application.
  • Validation and recovery awareness mattered more than a fast, irreversible change.
  • Proprietary schema, records, topology and maintenance commands could not become public.
  • No performance gain or financial saving could be inferred from storage reduction alone.

Responsibilities

I contributed to analyzing the storage condition, planning a production-safe intervention and checking the resulting footprint, with rollback readiness and service risk kept in view throughout.

Technical approach

The work followed a disciplined sequence: establish the before state, separate active workload needs from reclaimable allocation, classify the storage pressure, choose an intervention compatible with production constraints, prepare validation and recovery checks, execute within controlled boundaries, and confirm the after state. Locking, maintenance duration and service availability were part of the decision even though the exact employer procedure remains private.

Validation strategy

I compared the before and after footprints using the same GiB unit: approximately 199 GiB before and 133 GiB after. The storage result stands on its own; it is not presented as proof of latency, throughput, uptime or cost improvement.

Outcome

The production database footprint fell by 66 GiB, from approximately 199 GiB to 133 GiB—a reduction of about 33%.

Reliability and rollback considerations

The intervention was framed as a production change with prerequisite checks, an observed execution window, post-change validation and recovery awareness. Backup implications and restoration readiness were considered because a storage operation on a live database cannot be separated from recovery planning. The public narrative focuses on that discipline without publishing the employer's exact maintenance or backup procedures.

Security considerations

No customer records, SQL, table names, internal endpoints, credentials, backup topology or access procedures are included. The employer remains anonymized in this case study.

Lessons learned

Database efficiency work is strongest when the result stays narrow and auditable: measure the actual pressure, change only what is justified, confirm the result in the original unit and avoid attaching benefits that were not measured.
