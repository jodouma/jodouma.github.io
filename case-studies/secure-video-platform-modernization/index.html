Production engineering case study

Secure Multidomain Recording Platform — 100 Parallel Workers Validated

I progressed a JWT-secured Jitsi/Jibri recording platform from an initial five-worker baseline to 100 successfully validated parallel workers, with an architecture designed toward a future target of up to 300.

Context

An international online platform depended on self-hosted real-time collaboration and recording services. I was the primary implementer of the modernization layer and an operator and major contributor within the wider system; the open-source components and employer product were not my work alone.

Problem

The platform needed stronger access control, reliable meeting aliases across 12 production domains, automated certificate handling and a recording plane that could grow without routing internal service traffic unnecessarily across the public internet.

Constraints

  • Production changes crossed edge routing, container networking, authentication and recording workflows.
  • Access failures had to be tested as deliberately as successful sessions.
  • Cutover needed explicit apply, validation and rollback paths.
  • The completed 100-worker validation and future 300-worker target needed to remain visibly distinct.
  • Secrets, domains, addresses, routes, recordings and proprietary application integrations could not be exposed.

Architecture

Meeting clients reach Cloudflare-managed aliases across 12 production domains and an edge-protected Jitsi entry point, where authentication and JWT policy are enforced. Jibri workers sit behind a private network boundary and communicate with the conferencing platform through that private path. Registration and orchestration connect workers to recording jobs; exports leave through a controlled destination, while monitoring and operational procedures cover both planes. The system progressed from five initial workers to 100 successfully validated parallel workers. The architecture can evolve toward up to 300, which remains a future target rather than a completed benchmark.

Responsibilities

I implemented the modernization layer: strict JWT controls and guest-restriction negative tests, the 12-domain DNS rollout, DNS-01 certificate automation, host reverse-proxy cutover, private worker networking, Docker service rebinding, WebSocket handling, apply and rollback automation, worker validation, recording checks and export verification.

Technical approach

The architecture separates the public meeting entry point from the private recording-worker plane. Host-level Nginx terminates external traffic and WebSocket paths while Docker services bind to restricted interfaces. A private VLAN lets Jitsi and Jibri workers communicate directly without exposing internal recording traffic. Change scripts pair apply actions with rollback actions rather than treating reversal as an afterthought.

Validation strategy

Authentication validation covered accepted tokens and rejected missing, malformed or unauthorized cases. Five workers established the baseline for assignment, recording startup, completion, exports and repeatability. Horizontal expansion then checked registration, scheduling, resource behavior, assignment distribution, failed-to-join conditions, JWT propagation and export consistency until 100 parallel workers were successfully validated. Re-running deployment and export paths remained idempotent.

Outcome

The modernization established a secure, reversible recording plane and progressed from a five-worker proof baseline to 100 successfully validated parallel recording workers. The architecture can evolve toward up to 300 workers, but 300 remains a future scaling target rather than a completed capacity result.

Reliability and rollback considerations

Cutover documentation paired prerequisites, apply steps, health checks, negative tests and rollback procedures. Service rebinding and reverse-proxy changes were treated as reversible production changes, with a known working state retained while the worker plane evolved.

Security considerations

The release explains authentication and boundary hardening at a control-objective level only. It excludes token material, real domains, internal addresses, routes, topology, customer rooms, recordings, credentials and code that would expose the employer environment.

Lessons learned

Complex platform changes become safer when security failure paths, rollback and idempotency are first-class acceptance criteria. A scaling objective and an achieved validation result are different engineering statements.

Skills demonstrated

Linux and Nginx operations, Docker networking, private VLAN design, JWT authentication, DNS-01 certificate automation, WebSocket handling, production cutover, rollback design, negative testing, parallel workload validation, export reliability and operational documentation.
